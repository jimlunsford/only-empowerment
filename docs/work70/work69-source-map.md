## Every interaction state

All 13 states and their consequences are constructed educational illustrations. They are not quoted book passages, autobiographical events, personalized advice, predictions or a measurement of a learner's character. Every state is reachable by labeled buttons and represented in the script-free transcript.

| State ID | Teaching purpose | KPF support | Transitions |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `pressure` | Ordinary irritation creates a choice with sufficient capacity for a small responsibility. | Ch4 057-071; Ch7 159-181, 186-187 | `proof`, `overbuilt`, `miss`, `fatigue` |
| `proof` | One completed minimum creates a fact; repetition is required for later self-trust/identity. | Ch4 065-093; Ch7 173-182 | `fatigue`, `miss`, `overbuilt` |
| `overbuilt` | Expanding a task beyond sustainable capacity can crowd out follow-through. | Ch7 159-181 | `proof`, `fatigue`, `spiral` |

| `rest` | Completed communication, adjustment and sleep are distinct from an uncompleted deferred task. | Ch5 105-110; Ch7 186-187, 195-206 | `return`, `rest_escape`, `pressure` |
| `pushthrough` | Performing toughness by sacrificing needed sleep does not protect the standard. | Ch7 186-187, 198-206 | `rest`, `fatigue` |

The `proof`, `rest` and `correction` states each show all five authored stages. Their later-stage explanations explicitly depend on repeated evidence. They do not claim that one household action immediately rebuilds self-trust or stabilizes identity. The explicit rest branch preserves an open obligation: a deferred task is still uncompleted until addressed.

## Quotation and adaptation ledger

No narrative book paragraph is reproduced as a quotation in the teaching copy or scenario. The five stage names and five failure-point headings intentionally preserve the author's exact wording and are identified as authored language in the provenance statement. All surrounding explanations, reflection prompts and household situations are newly drafted adaptations.

The exact full sequence is shown because its language and order identify the framework: Pressure creates the choice; Action creates proof; Proof rebuilds self-trust; Self-trust stabilizes identity; Stable identity reduces negotiation. Source: Ch3 paragraph 051 and Ch4 paragraphs 055 and 093. Attribution: Jim Lunsford, The Discipline Loop.

## Deliberate boundaries and source gaps

The KPF supplies the full mechanism, all five authored break points, correction sequence, rest/adjustment distinction and explicit support/medical-help acknowledgment. There is no missing manuscript section blocking the educational experience.

Editorial boundaries add clarity without claiming to be new authored doctrines: hardship is not all chosen; accommodations and medical care are not weakness; the experience is not a treatment plan; a household example cannot measure relapse severity; UI choices are not real-world proof. Those boundaries follow the assignment and the manuscript's treatment of real conditions, help and non-reckless action. They are not presented as clinical efficacy claims.

The exact household details are invented for teaching and labeled accordingly. A different possible outcome in real life does not invalidate the lesson, because the scenario demonstrates the mechanism rather than predicting consequences. No memories or third-party private stories were imported.

The KPF is sufficient for source fidelity. Future implementation still needs founder copy/experience approval and scoped source, route and hosting work. Those are release decisions, not manuscript access gaps.
