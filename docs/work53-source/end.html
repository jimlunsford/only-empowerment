Everyday application

Build a Life You Do Not Want to Escape

What has your work made it possible to stay for?

Rebuilding has a purpose beyond keeping a good record. Maybe you can sit at the table through an uncomfortable conversation. Maybe a job ends and you can tell somebody what happened instead of spending weeks pretending you still go to work. Grief can still hurt. There can still be an appointment on the calendar and someone giving you a ride. The difference shows up in what you can face and what still gets a say in your next decision.

An evening of sleep, quiet or company can help you come back to something hard tomorrow. Night after night of going silent so you never have to face the same conversation does a different job. Look at what the relief lets you return to and what keeps waiting for you.

Think about two fictional people. One loses a job when the shop closes. The closure was outside his control. A line he can draw is to answer honestly when his partner asks how the job search went. Another has had the same job for years and realizes she scrolls through every lunch she spends with a friend. Her first line might be to put the phone away during the meal. A crisis and a quiet loss of attention are both places to start.

Fictional paper note for Thursday lunch. “When we sit down, I’ll put my phone in my bag. If I pull it out without thinking, I’ll put it back. After lunch I can say whether I did that. My friend gets to decide what she wants to talk about.” That note is usable even if nobody ever sees it.

Either person can prepare a line on paper. Only the next conversation or lunch can give that line a record. Repeated conduct can earn trust in your word, and trust can guide the choice you make when nobody asks. That is how these parts belong to one life.

Take one question with you

One part of your life
Pick a situation you want to be present for. What keeps getting between you and it? Which part can you act on?
A line with a place in your day
Say what you will do and when the next real opportunity comes. What needs to be ready, moved, asked for or protected? Include help or rest if the day requires it.
Something you could honestly point to
Before you act, name what would count as having done it. If you miss, what concrete return or repair would be possible? A person affected can still choose how to respond.
Come back after the day
What actually happened? If all you have is an intention, leave the record there. If you carried it out or made a correction, name precisely that. What could you reasonably trust yourself to do next?

You can take one question with you, in your head or on paper. Keep the answer yours. The next place this work happens is in your day.
