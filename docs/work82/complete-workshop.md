## Pure Execution Mode

Address: `#/execution`

Carry a sound decision into the work.

Illustration: you borrowed a set of tools and promised to return the complete set before the owner needs it Sunday evening. It is Saturday. You have time and can safely gather the tools, but the handoff is not arranged. You keep finding other things to straighten in the garage.

Knowing what you owe does not return it. A standard is the responsibility or value your action serves. Here, it is keeping your agreement and respecting the owner’s time and property.

First, watch the five steps carry this responsibility through. Then take over unfinished decisions, respond when the facts change, and try a different situation with less guidance. You can compare explanations, skip practice or leave at any point.

The method does not require a special feeling. It requires judgment and appropriate action. Missing permission, unsafe conditions and changed capacity still matter. Help, rest or a stop can be the action the standard requires.

Jim developed this teaching through rebuilding ordinary life after addiction and leading recovery groups. These situations are illustrations, not accounts of his life.

## A request is not an agreement

The message has been sent. Decide what that allows you to claim or do.

### What follows from the unanswered request?

**Plan to leave it at the owner’s door at four.**

Consequence: You have a convenient drop-off plan, but no permission for that arrangement.

Reasoning: Silence does not authorize a drop-off. The return must respect the owner’s answer and property, not just your preferred finish time.

Consequence: Your request is complete. The tools remain with you and the return is still open.

Reasoning: Naming the whole job finished hides its dependency and the delivery still owed. Give the request its proper credit without inflating it.

Consequence: The set stays ready while agreement is pending. You can attend to other suitable responsibilities.

Reasoning: Waiting on a real answer is different from avoiding an available action. Follow up through an appropriate route when timing requires it; use the answer when it arrives.

## Safe access is missing

Address: `#/execution/capacity`

Make the pause do useful work.

Illustration: you used the permitted message route and agreed on a later handoff. Before leaving, you find a heavy object blocking the stored tool case. Moving it alone would be unsafe. You can contact someone for appropriate help and message the owner.

The return remains owed. That does not make unsafe effort responsible. A needed stop or request for help must become something you actually carry out.

### What fits these facts?

**Move the obstruction alone to keep the promised time.**

Consequence: You attempt effort the situation has established as unsafe. The promise has not supplied safe access.

Reasoning: Keeping the time does not justify ignoring the obstacle. Stop that effort and seek appropriate assistance.

**Stop the unsafe effort, request help and explain the delay.**

Consequence: The unsafe attempt stops. Help has been requested, and the owner knows the handoff may need adjustment. Access is not yet restored.

Reasoning: Those actions protect the responsibility. A request is not a helper’s arrival or a new agreement. Follow up and resume only when the conditions support it.

**Rest and leave the owner expecting the agreed handoff.**

Consequence: You stop the effort, but the owner still expects a return you may not be able to make.

Reasoning: Rest can be appropriate without being earned. Where contact is available, an affected commitment still needs an honest update and adjustment.

### Complete explanation

Help, rest and stopping belong inside the method. They are not loopholes or punishments. In the demonstration’s responsible continuation, help makes access safe, the owner agrees to the revised time, and the complete set is returned.
